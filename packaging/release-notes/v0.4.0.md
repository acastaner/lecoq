This release is about speed. It follows up on a performance and bloat review of the whole app:
nothing changes about how lecoq looks or what it does, but moving around a folder, editing, and
transforming images are all lighter and smoother.

## Snappier navigation between images

- Switching to the next or previous image in a folder no longer re-converts the picture on the UI
  thread each time — the display-size cache now holds pixels that are ready to hand straight to the
  GPU. Flicking through a folder stays responsive even on large images.
- lecoq finds an image's place in its folder directly instead of re-scanning the whole listing on
  every frame, so navigation cost no longer grows with the size of the folder.

## Smoother pan and zoom on annotated images

- Panning and zooming an image with annotations on it now reuses a composite built once at your
  display's resolution, instead of re-rendering every annotation on every frame. Dragging around a
  heavily annotated screenshot is markedly smoother. (See ADR 0011 for the design.)
- The composite is drawn straight into the texture's pixels, dropping several full-image copies per
  frame.

## Faster rotate and flip

- Rotating and flipping large images is substantially quicker — the pixels are moved a row at a
  time instead of one at a time. On a 100-megapixel image, a 90° rotation is about 43% faster and a
  vertical flip about 64% faster.

## Lighter under the hood

- The colour transform for an animation is built once when it loads rather than rebuilt on every
  frame, and a good deal of per-frame allocation and copying (tooltips, text layout, style and
  history snapshots, config comparison) has been removed. The result is lower CPU and memory use
  while you browse and edit, with no visible change in behaviour.

Cold-start and large-folder first-paint times are unchanged from 0.3.0 (both well within budget).
